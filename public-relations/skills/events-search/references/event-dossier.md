# The finalist dossier

Only finalists get this treatment. The goal is a page the user can act on without
opening a browser: what it costs, when it closes, and who to call.

Start at the organizer's own site, and specifically its vendor, exhibitor, or
sponsor page — that is where the numbers are. Aggregator listings fill gaps at
best.

## Fields to capture

**Identity**
- Event name, and the year or edition (e.g. "38th annual")
- One-line description of what it actually is
- Organizer: organisation name
- Official URL, and the direct vendor/sponsor page URL

**When and where**
- Exact dates and daily hours
- Venue name and full address
- Distance and drive time from the business address
- Indoor, outdoor, or both
- First-year event? Note it.

**Scale**
- Attendance, with the year it refers to and the source
- Number of vendors or exhibitors, if published
- Vendor-to-attendee ratio where both are known

**Money**
- Booth cost, by size and by type (10x10, 10x20, food, non-profit rate)
- What the fee includes: table, chairs, tent, power, wifi, listing, tickets
- Sponsorship tiers, with price and what each includes
- Which tiers include booth space
- Additional costs: electricity, tent rental, insurance certificate, permit,
  city or health licence, parking, load-in fee

**Deadlines and status**
- Vendor application deadline
- Sponsorship commitment deadline
- Whether applications are currently **open**, **not yet open**, or **closed**
- Whether acceptance is juried, first-come, or automatic
- Waitlist status if full

**Contact**
- Organizer contact name and role
- Email and phone
- The exact page or document the contact came from

**Rules and fit constraints**
- Category exclusivity: available, sold, or not offered
- Product restrictions, sampling and food rules, live-animal rules
- Insurance requirement and coverage minimum
- Setup and teardown times, vehicle access, whether staff must stay all day
- Power availability and whether generators are allowed
- Anything that would disqualify this specific business

**Evidence of value**
- Past vendor or sponsor list, if public
- Direct competitors present in past years
- Press coverage or photos indicating real turnout

## Where these fields actually live

| Looking for | Go here |
|---|---|
| Booth price | "Vendor application" or "exhibitor prospectus" PDF |
| Sponsorship tiers | "Sponsorship packet" or "Become a sponsor" PDF |
| Deadlines | The application form itself, not the summary page |
| Organizer contact | Contact page, PDF footer, or the form's reply-to |
| Real attendance | Local news coverage of last year's event |
| Past vendors | Last year's event program, map, or sponsor logo wall |
| Rules | Vendor terms and conditions, usually page 2 of the application |

## Traps

- **Last year's PDF is still up.** Check the year on every document. A prospectus
  with no year on it is `[unconfirmed]` until the date matches.
- **The price on the summary page is the early-bird price.** Capture the tiers
  and the date the early rate ends.
- **"Free booth" usually means non-profit only.** Read the eligibility line.
- **A sold-out event is still worth listing** — with next year's expected
  application window, so it can be caught in time.
- **The form is the source of truth for deadlines.** Summary pages go stale;
  application forms get updated because they have to work.
- **No published price is a legitimate outcome.** Record the organizer's contact
  and say "price on application." Do not estimate one.
