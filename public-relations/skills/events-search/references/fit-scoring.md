# Fit scoring

The point of the score is to make a spending decision defensible, not to produce
a precise number. Score fast, shortlist, then spend the real effort on the
dossier.

## Disqualifiers — check these first, cheaply

An event is out, with a one-line reason, if:

- The date has passed, or the vendor application closed before today.
- Vendors or sponsors are not accepted at all.
- The business is excluded by rule — category exclusivity already sold, a
  competing sponsor holding the category, a venue policy that bars the product,
  or a licensing requirement the business does not hold.
- It is outside the ring by enough that staffing it is implausible, unless it is
  exceptional (include it, flag the drive time).
- It conflicts with a blackout date in the parameters file.
- The parameters file records it as already worked and declined.

## The rubric

Seven criteria, scored 1–5, weighted. Total out of 100.

| Criterion | Weight | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Audience overlap** | 25 | The attendee is the customer — same demographic, same need, present with intent | Wrong age, wrong income, wrong geography, no plausible need |
| **Reach** | 20 | Thousands of attendees, verified from a recent year | A few dozen, or attendance unknown and unknowable |
| **Cost to reach** | 15 | Low cost per qualified attendee; sponsorship includes booth | Premium price for a modest, unqualified crowd |
| **Vendor access quality** | 15 | Real dwell time, good placement, attendees browsing booths by design | Booth is scenery at an event nobody attends for the vendors |
| **Competitive field** | 10 | No direct competitor, or exclusivity available | Ten of the same business, all discounting |
| **Brand fit** | 10 | The event's character flatters the brand and matches its cautions | Association the client would wince at |
| **Operational feasibility** | 5 | One person, one day, easy load-in, power available | Three days, overnight staffing, no power, no vehicle access |

**Bands:** 75+ pursue now · 55–74 shortlist · 40–54 watch for next year ·
under 40 pass.

## The two lanes

Take finalists from both, and say which lane each is in.

**Category-native.** High intent per head. Also: expensive, crowded with direct
competitors, and sometimes mostly vendors selling to each other. Do not
automatically rank these first because the fit is obvious.

**General community.** A festival, a school carnival, a charity run. Lower intent
per head, an order of magnitude more heads, a fraction of the cost, and the
goodwill of showing up locally. For most local service businesses this lane is
where the return actually is — and for a business with no category-native event
within the ring, it is the whole strategy.

## Booth or sponsorship — they are different buys

Score and recommend the right product, not just the event:

- **Booth** buys conversations, demos, lead capture, and sampling. Worth it where
  attendees browse and dwell.
- **Sponsorship** buys logo placement, stage mentions, program listing, and
  association with a cause. Worth it at events with no vendor row, and at charity
  events where the association is the product.
- Many sponsor tiers **include** booth space, and that is often the cheaper route
  to the booth. Always check the tier sheet before quoting a standalone booth
  price.

## Judgement calls worth writing down

- **Recurrence beats size once.** A weekly farmers market at $30 a session can
  out-earn one $900 festival booth, and it compounds recognition.
- **Attendance figures are marketing.** An organizer's "50,000 attendees" is
  usually a gate count across three days, or a guess. Note the source and the
  year; prefer a news report over a prospectus.
- **Vendor-to-attendee ratio is the pay-to-play tell.** 200 vendors and 2,000
  attendees is a room selling to itself. If the ratio cannot be established, say
  so and do not infer reach.
- **Weather and season are real costs.** An outdoor August event in the South
  needs a tent, water, and a shift rotation. Fold that into feasibility.
- **First-year events are a gamble.** Cheap, uncrowded, and unproven. Worth a
  small bet, never a flagship one — and label them as first-year.
