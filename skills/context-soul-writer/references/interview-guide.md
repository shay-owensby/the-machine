# Soul Interview and Output Guide

Use this guide as a coverage map, not a questionnaire. Ask only the next most useful question, branch from the answer, and mark a category resolved only when its guidance is observable enough to affect a draft.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. The user's live rewrites and corrections.
2. Writing the user identifies as unmistakably theirs.
3. Repeated phrases, stories, and speech patterns from the interview.
4. Explicit preferences supported by concrete examples or contrasts.
5. Existing approved brand material.
6. Inferences, which must remain labeled until confirmed.

Record whether a rule is confirmed, inferred, provisional, or unresolved when that distinction matters.

## Core Interview Coverage

### 1. Voice purpose

Establish what the voice must accomplish, what relationship it creates, and how the reader should think and feel afterward. Take the audience, positioning, and intended conversion action from `about.md`; ask only how the voice should affect that audience and support that action.

Useful probes:

- What should people say about the brand after reading three pieces?
- Is the brand primarily a guide, peer, challenger, advocate, host, expert, or something else?
- What emotional state is the reader in before and after the writing?

### 2. Authentic voice evidence

Collect representative writing or speech: emails, posts, transcripts, notes, sales messages, explanations, stories, or rewrites. Ask what the user likes and dislikes in each. Distinguish the founder's natural voice from an intentionally constructed company voice.

Useful probes:

- Show me something you wrote that sounds exactly like you. Which lines prove it?
- What have others written for you that you would never say?
- Rewrite this sentence as you would actually say it aloud.

### 3. Personality with boundaries

Identify three to five durable voice pillars. For each pillar, define observable behaviors, the desired effect, and an explicit “but not” boundary. Reject interchangeable labels without evidence.

Example shape: “Direct: leads with the point and names tradeoffs, but never shames the reader or fakes certainty.”

### 4. Reader relationship and stance

Clarify authority, intimacy, vulnerability, optimism, humor, urgency, and persuasion. Determine when the brand teaches, tells, asks, challenges, reassures, sells, or admits uncertainty.

### 5. Diction and stylistic vocabulary

Capture favored stylistic words, signature phrases, metaphors, cultural references, vocabulary level, acceptable slang, profanity boundaries, inclusive language, ways of addressing the reader, and words that are banned or overused for stylistic reasons. Take official brand, offer, and product names and required capitalization from `about.md`; do not duplicate them here.

### 6. Syntax, rhythm, and mechanics

Determine sentence and paragraph length, pacing, fragments, contractions, active voice, headings, lists, questions, asides, punctuation, emojis, capitalization, numerals, and formatting preferences. Separate hard rules from tendencies.

### 7. Story, proof, and persuasion

Learn how the brand opens, explains, tells stories, uses examples, presents proof, phrases calls to action, and balances emotional and rational appeals. Take the proof inventory, approved factual claims, and CTA destinations from `about.md`. Capture only their presentation style plus persuasion tactics that feel manipulative, inflated, or off-brand.

### 8. Humor and emotional range

Define the kinds of humor, intensity, candor, warmth, irreverence, and vulnerability that fit. Identify sensitive moments where the tone must narrow. Ask for examples of jokes or emotional language that cross the line.

### 9. Channel and situation calibration

Using the channels and audiences recorded in `about.md`, map what remains invariant and what changes across relevant situations—for example website, email, sales, social, support, long-form education, launches, apologies, or crisis communication. Also test cold versus warm audiences and low- versus high-stakes claims. Do not re-interview for the channel plan itself.

Do not invent channel guidance for unused channels.

### 10. Anti-voice and guardrails

Collect disliked brands or styles, common clichés, artificial AI-like habits, manipulative rhetorical moves, ethical lines, and language that would damage trust. Factual claim restrictions and prohibited business topics belong in `about.md`; reference them rather than copying them. Convert stylistic dislikes into precise prohibitions or warnings.

### 11. Signature moves

Identify recurring openings, transitions, rhetorical devices, analogies, story shapes, closings, calls to action, or verbal quirks that make the writing recognizable. Avoid forcing signature elements when the evidence does not support them.

### 12. Calibration and edge cases

Test the emerging model with short, realistic passages that span at least two meaningfully different situations. Invite line edits. Probe any case where two rules compete, such as directness versus empathy or expertise versus accessibility.

## Follow-up Triggers

Do not accept an answer as complete when it:

- relies only on broad adjectives;
- combines opposites without explaining when each applies;
- states a preference but gives no example or boundary;
- conflicts with a sample, prior answer, or `about.md`;
- describes aspiration while the user's authentic examples show something else;
- gives a universal rule that likely changes by channel or stakes;
- copies a reference brand without identifying what is truly transferable;
- says “anything is fine,” “you know what I mean,” or similar without enough detail to guide a writer.

Follow up with examples, forced contrasts, rewrites, scales, exceptions, or a short test passage. Stop probing a category once the rule is usable and confirmed.

## `soul.md` Content Model

Adapt headings to the brand, but ensure the finished file covers these functions:

1. **Document status** — purpose, ISO `last_confirmed` date, relationship to `_context/about.md`, evidence reviewed, and any provisional areas.
2. **Voice at a glance** — a compact description of the voice, reader relationship, desired effect, and strongest anti-voice warning.
3. **Voice pillars** — three to five distinctive principles, each with behaviors and “but not” boundaries.
4. **Tone range** — invariant identity plus calibrated changes by relevant audience, channel, situation, emotion, or stakes.
5. **Language** — stylistic vocabulary, ways of addressing the reader, signature phrases, vocabulary level, metaphors, slang or profanity policy, and stylistic avoid list; defer official names and required terminology to `about.md`.
6. **Rhythm and mechanics** — sentence, paragraph, structure, punctuation, formatting, and stylistic tendencies; distinguish rules from preferences.
7. **Story and persuasion** — openings, explanation style, how proof is presented, emotional register, CTA phrasing, and ethical or stylistic guardrails; defer proof inventory, approved claims, and CTA destinations to `about.md`.
8. **Signature moves** — recurring techniques that are supported by evidence.
9. **Do / don't examples** — paired examples and annotated calibration passages that show how rules change actual copy.
10. **Drafting checklist** — a short preflight another writer can use before delivering work.
11. **Open questions** — skipped, unknown, contradictory, provisional, or stale items, with the next useful question for each.

Avoid restating the full business description, offers, audiences, or operational facts from `about.md`. Link or refer to that file as the factual source of truth.

## Final Read-back

Before writing, summarize:

- the one-sentence voice definition;
- the voice pillars and their boundaries;
- the most important mechanics and vocabulary rules;
- how tone shifts in relevant contexts;
- the strongest anti-voice warnings;
- unresolved or deliberately skipped areas.

Ask the user to correct the model and approve the write. After writing, verify that every strong claim is supported by confirmed evidence or clearly labeled as provisional.
