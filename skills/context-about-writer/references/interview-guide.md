# Business Context Interview Guide

Use this as a coverage checklist and adaptive question bank. Ask only questions relevant to the business, one small cluster at a time. The examples are prompts to choose from, not a script to recite.

## Core Coverage

### 1. Identity and Business Overview

- Legal business name, public-facing name, and preferred spelling or capitalization
- What the business does in plain language and the category it belongs to
- Founding story, year founded, ownership or leadership facts relevant to public writing
- Business model: local service, professional service, retail, ecommerce, software, nonprofit, franchise, marketplace, or hybrid
- Website, primary contact channels, and the single best public call to action

Probe unclear category labels by asking what a customer actually buys and what outcome they receive.

### 2. Offers and Buying Process

- Products, services, packages, memberships, or programs currently offered
- Flagship or highest-priority offer and any seasonal or discontinued offers
- What each offer includes, excludes, costs, and how pricing is communicated
- Typical engagement size, duration, lead time, capacity, and fulfillment method
- How a prospect becomes a customer: inquiry, consultation, quote, booking, checkout, contract, onboarding
- Guarantees, warranties, financing, refunds, cancellation rules, minimums, or eligibility requirements

Distinguish current offers from planned ones. Capture exact names and approved descriptions.

### 3. Ideal Customer Profile

- Best-fit customer type: consumer or organization; industry, company size, life stage, role, budget, or other meaningful traits
- Geography, language, accessibility, and channel preferences
- Primary problem, desired outcome, urgency, and trigger that starts the search
- Buyer, decision-maker, user, influencer, and gatekeeper when these differ
- Common objections, fears, failed alternatives, and decision criteria
- Most profitable, successful, enjoyable, or strategically important segments
- Poor-fit customers, disqualifiers, and work the business does not accept

Do not rely on demographics alone. Seek observable situations, behaviors, needs, and buying context.

### 4. Locations, Service Areas, and Delivery

- Physical, mailing, showroom, office, warehouse, or appointment-only locations; label each accurately
- Areas served using exact cities, neighborhoods, counties, states, countries, postal codes, radii, or travel limits
- In-person, mobile, delivery, shipping, remote, or hybrid availability
- Travel fees, delivery zones, shipping restrictions, virtual-service boundaries, and location-specific offers
- Landmark, parking, accessibility, entry, or appointment details useful to customers

Resolve fuzzy phrases such as “the metro area” or “nationwide” into meaningful boundaries and exceptions.

### 5. Hours and Availability

- Regular business, phone, support, delivery, and appointment hours, with time zone
- Whether hours differ by location, service, channel, or season
- Holiday closures, emergency or after-hours coverage, response times, and booking cutoffs
- Walk-in versus appointment-only rules and current capacity constraints

Record “by appointment” or “varies” precisely rather than converting it into fixed hours.

### 6. Positioning and Proof

- Why customers choose the business and what makes its approach meaningfully different
- Alternatives customers compare, including doing nothing or handling it themselves
- Competitors the user is comfortable naming and how comparisons should be framed
- Credentials, licenses, certifications, awards, partnerships, years of experience, process strengths, or proprietary methods
- Testimonials, reviews, case studies, measurable outcomes, client counts, or recognizable customers that may be cited
- Claims that are approved, restricted, outdated, confidential, or still need substantiation

Ask for evidence and dates when a claim is likely to appear in public copy. Never upgrade an anecdote into a universal promise.

### 7. Messaging Facts and Conversion Paths

- Official brand, product, service, package, and audience names, including exact spelling and capitalization
- Plain-language category terms and factual descriptions the business approves
- Message priorities by offer or audience: which benefit, problem, differentiator, or proof point matters most
- Primary conversion action by audience or funnel stage and its actual destination, such as a booking link, inquiry form, phone number, or checkout
- Topics, comparisons, promises, or claims restricted for business, legal, compliance, or reputational reasons

Keep this factual and strategic. Do not ask about voice attributes, tone, humor, formality, rhythm, stylistic word preferences, or writing examples; those belong in `soul.md`. Store the required action and destination here, while `soul.md` governs how a call to action sounds.

### 8. Operational and Policy Facts

- Contact routes, booking links, response expectations, payment methods, and customer-support process
- Service prerequisites, intake requirements, age limits, accessibility accommodations, and language support
- Privacy, safety, regulatory, insurance, licensing, or disclosure details relevant to public messaging
- Promotions, discount rules, referral programs, subscriptions, and loyalty programs

Capture only what future writers may need; do not solicit private operational secrets.

## Extended Coverage

Use these categories when the user's future writing needs would benefit:

- business goals, growth priorities, launches, seasonality, and capacity;
- content goals, channels, formats, publishing cadence, and conversion measures;
- audience awareness stages and funnel-specific factual priorities;
- founder or team bios, culture, values, community involvement, and causes;
- media, partnership, recruiting, investor, or internal-communications context;
- SEO topics, local search phrasing, industry terminology, and frequently asked questions;
- crisis, reputation, or sensitive-topic boundaries.

## Follow-Up Patterns

Use focused follow-ups when an answer is:

- **Broad:** “Which three services account for most inquiries today?”
- **Relative:** “When you say affordable, what range or comparison is accurate?”
- **Geographically vague:** “Which cities or counties are definitely inside and outside that area?”
- **Unverified:** “Is that an approved public claim, an estimate, or something we should verify?”
- **Inconsistent:** “Earlier you said appointments end at 5 p.m.; does the 24/7 availability apply only to emergency calls?”
- **Overloaded:** Split it into offer, audience, or location-specific facts before continuing.

## Completion Audit

Before finalizing, check that:

- every core category has a confirmed status;
- names, URLs, addresses, locations, time zones, hours, prices, and dates are exact where available;
- current, planned, seasonal, and discontinued information are separated;
- service-area boundaries and delivery modes are unambiguous;
- ideal and poor-fit customers are concrete enough to guide writing;
- claims and proof have a source and verification status;
- voice and style guidance has not been duplicated from `soul.md`;
- contradictions and open questions are listed rather than silently resolved;
- the user has reviewed the highest-impact facts and had a final chance to add anything important.
