# Platform Limitations

Hard limits, truncation points, and writing targets for every platform this skill
writes for. **Hard limits** are enforced by the platform — exceed them and the post
is rejected or silently cut. **Targets** are what the caption should actually aim
for. Never write to the hard limit just because it exists.

Verified August 2026. Platforms change these without notice — if a caption is
rejected for length, trust the platform over this file and flag it for an update.

---

## Universal rules

Apply these on every platform before the platform-specific rules below.

1. **Write to the truncation point, not the cap.** Every platform hides the tail of
   a caption behind a "see more" link. The visible window is the only text most
   people ever read. The hook, the value, and the reason to keep reading all belong
   inside it.
2. **Never split a sentence across the fold.** If the truncation point lands
   mid-thought, rewrite so a complete idea ends before it.
3. **Front-load the call to action's subject, not the CTA itself.** "Link in bio"
   before the fold wastes the window. The reason to want the link goes there.
4. **Count everything.** Spaces, line breaks, punctuation, emoji, @mentions,
   hashtags, and URLs all consume characters. Emoji frequently count as 2+
   characters, and some (skin-tone or flag sequences) count as 4–8.
5. **Leave 5% headroom.** Write to 95% of any hard limit so a late edit, an added
   emoji, or a platform counting quirk never truncates the post.
6. **One idea per caption.** Length limits are not the constraint that matters most
   — attention is. A caption that fits is not the same as a caption worth reading.
7. **Line breaks are formatting, not decoration.** Break every 1–2 sentences.
   Walls of text lose readers on every platform in this list.
8. **No platform supports rich text.** Bold, italics, and bullets do not exist in
   captions. Unicode "bold" characters break screen readers — never use them.

## Facebook

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Post / caption | 63,206 characters |
| Reels caption | 63,206 characters |
| Comment | 8,000 characters |
| Page bio | 101 characters |
| Page name | 75 characters |
| Photo alt text | ~1,000 characters |
| Link preview headline (organic) | ~100 characters, truncates ~25–40 |

### Truncation

Desktop feed cuts at approximately **477 characters**. Mobile cuts far earlier —
between **150 and 200 characters**, and mobile is where most of the audience is.
Treat 150 as the real fold.

### Write to

**40–150 characters** for a standard post. Short posts materially outperform long
ones here; posts in the 40–80 character range see the highest engagement. Facebook
is the one platform in this list where brevity is close to an absolute rule.

Exceptions where longer is correct:
- **Story-driven posts** (community updates, founder notes) — up to 500 characters,
  with the first 150 carrying a complete hook.
- **Facebook Reels** — 40–100 characters. The video carries the message; the caption
  supplements it.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** none enforced in practice.
- **Recommended:** 0–2. Hashtags do very little on Facebook and more than two reads
  as spam to the algorithm and the audience.
- **Placement:** inline or at the end of the caption. Facebook does not surface
  first-comment hashtags — never put them there.

### Links and mentions

- URLs count toward the character total at full length and are **not** shortened.
- Posting a bare link generates a preview card; the URL can be deleted from the
  caption after the preview loads, and the card stays.
- Link-in-caption posts are down-ranked relative to native content. Prefer native
  media with the link in the first comment when reach matters more than clicks.
- @mentions of Pages count toward the character total.

### Rules

- Put a complete, self-contained hook in the first 150 characters.
- Ask one question maximum. Two questions in a caption splits the comment thread.
- Emoji are safe and lift engagement here; 1–3 per post.
- Never use ALL CAPS for more than one word — Facebook's spam classifiers weight it.

## Instagram

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Caption (feed, Reels, carousel) | 2,200 characters |
| Hashtags per post | 30 (caption + first comment combined) |
| @mentions per post | 20 |
| Comment | 2,200 characters |
| Bio | 150 characters |
| Username | 30 characters |
| Display name | 30 characters |
| Alt text | 100 characters |
| Story text (per text box) | ~250 characters |

### Truncation

- **Feed:** 125 characters, then "… more". On narrow mobile viewports it can cut
  slightly earlier — write to 125 and assume less.
- **Reels tab:** only **55–60 characters** are visible. This is the tightest window
  of any surface in this document.
- **Explore / grid:** no caption shown at all.

### Write to

- **Feed post:** 138–150 characters total for a punchy caption, or up to 1,000 for a
  long-form storytelling caption — but only when the first 125 characters justify
  the tap. There is no middle ground that performs well; either be short or commit
  to a full piece of writing.
- **Reels:** 40–60 characters. One line. If it does not fit in the Reels-tab window,
  it does not exist.
- **Carousel:** treat like a feed post, but the first 125 characters should tell the
  reader why to swipe.
- **Stories:** 1–8 words per text element. Never a paragraph.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** 30 per post, counted across the caption *and* the first comment
  together. 20 in the caption plus 10 in a comment hits the cap.
- **Recommended:** 3–5 highly specific, relevant tags. Instagram's own guidance and
  the engagement data both favour a small, precise set over a block of 30.
- **Placement:** in-caption is now preferred — first-comment hashtags no longer carry
  a reach penalty advantage and the caption keeps them associated with the post for
  search. If they hurt the visual, put them after three line breaks at the end.
- Never repeat an identical hashtag block across consecutive posts.

### Links and mentions

- **Captions are not clickable.** URLs render as plain text and still consume
  characters. Do not put a URL in an Instagram caption — it is dead weight.
- @mentions and #hashtags *are* clickable in captions.
- Stories support link stickers; the sticker text is limited to ~30 characters.

### Rules

- Line breaks survive on Instagram. Use them — a 4-line caption outperforms the same
  words as one block.
- Alt text is 100 characters and is a hard accessibility requirement on every image
  post. Describe the image; do not repeat the caption or stuff keywords.
- Instagram search indexes caption text. Include the plain-language phrase someone
  would search for, written as a normal sentence, not as a keyword list.
- Emoji count as 2+ characters each against the 2,200 cap.

## X

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Post (free account) | 280 characters |
| Post (X Premium) | 25,000 characters |
| Direct message | 10,000 characters |
| Bio | 160 characters |
| Display name | 50 characters |
| Username / handle | 15 characters |
| Image alt text | 1,000 characters |
| Poll option | 25 characters |

### Truncation

- A free post is fully visible at 280.
- A Premium long post shows the **first 280 characters** in the timeline followed by
  "Show more". The full text opens only on the post page. Treat 280 as the fold even
  on a Premium account — a long post whose first 280 characters do not stand alone
  will not get the tap.

### Write to

**71–100 characters** for a standard post. Posts in this range consistently earn the
most engagement; the extra room up to 280 is for context, not for filling.

For threads: each post stands alone at 280. The first post must work as a complete
statement — most readers never reach post two.

### Character counting quirks

- **Every URL counts as 23 characters**, regardless of actual length. A 200-character
  URL and a 15-character URL both cost 23.
- **Most emoji count as 2 characters.** Composite emoji (skin tones, flags, family
  sequences) cost more.
- CJK characters count as 2.
- @mentions at the very start of a reply do not count toward the 280.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** none enforced, but more than a handful triggers spam filtering.
- **Recommended:** 1–2, maximum. X is the platform where hashtag overuse is most
  visibly punished.
- **Placement:** inline where the word naturally falls, or at the end. Never a block.

### Rules

- No link in the first post of a thread if reach matters — link-bearing posts are
  down-ranked. Put the link in a reply.
- Do not open with an @mention unless it is a genuine reply; it limits visibility to
  mutual followers.
- One idea per post. If it needs two ideas, it needs two posts.
- Alt text (1,000 characters) is required on every image.

## TikTok

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Caption | 4,000 characters (raised from 2,200) |
| Photo mode / carousel caption | 4,000 characters |
| Bio | 80 characters |
| Display name | 30 characters |
| Username | 24 characters |
| Comment | 150 characters |
| On-screen text (per text element) | ~100 characters |

> **Caveat:** the increase from 2,200 to 4,000 has not rolled out uniformly — older
> accounts, some regions, and most third-party schedulers still enforce 2,200. If the
> caption is being published through a scheduling tool, **stay under 2,200**. Only
> exceed it when posting natively and the composer accepts it.

### Truncation

Roughly **70–100 characters** before the caption collapses behind "more". The video
is playing while the caption is read, so the effective attention window is even
smaller than the character window.

### Write to

**80–150 characters.** One line of hook, then optional keyword-bearing context.

The 4,000-character ceiling exists for search, not for reading. If a longer caption
is being written for discoverability, the structure is:

1. **First 70 characters:** the hook. Complete thought, no dangling clause.
2. **Next 100–200:** context and the searchable phrase, written as plain sentences.
3. **End:** hashtags.

Nobody reads past line one. Everything after it is for TikTok Search.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** bounded only by the caption character count.
- **Recommended:** 3–5. Mix one broad tag, two niche tags, and one branded or
  campaign tag.
- **Placement:** end of the caption, after the copy. Hashtags count against the
  caption limit.
- Avoid #fyp, #foryou, and #viral — they carry no ranking weight and consume the
  window.

### Rules

- TikTok is a search engine. Write the caption in the words a person would type into
  the search bar, as a natural sentence.
- On-screen text and the caption should not duplicate each other. On-screen text
  hooks; the caption contextualises.
- Never write a caption that only makes sense with sound on.
- Photo mode captions follow the same rules as video captions.

## Pinterest

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Pin title | 100 characters |
| Pin description | 500 characters |
| Board name | 50 characters |
| Board description | 500 characters |
| Alt text | 500 characters |
| Profile display name | 30 characters |
| Profile about | 500 characters |

### Truncation

- **Pin title:** approximately **40 characters** visible in the feed grid; the full
  100 shows on the Pin detail page.
- **Pin description:** only the first **50–60 characters** appear in feed. The rest
  requires opening the Pin.

### Write to

- **Title:** 40–60 characters. Front-load the primary keyword. The title is the
  single highest-leverage field on Pinterest.
- **Description:** 100–200 characters of natural, keyword-bearing sentences. The
  first 50–60 must carry the main search term.
- **Alt text:** a genuine description of the image, 100–200 characters. Distinct from
  the description — do not paste the same text into both.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** 20 per Pin.
- **Recommended:** 0–3. Pinterest has actively deprioritised hashtags; keywords in
  natural sentences do the work instead.
- **Placement:** end of the description if used at all.

### Rules

- Pinterest is a visual search engine. Every field is a ranking signal — write for
  the searcher, not for a follower.
- Never write a title in title case with filler words. "Easy 20-Minute Weeknight
  Pasta" beats "Our Favourite Thing To Make On A Busy Night".
- The description should answer "what will I get if I click", not describe the image
  (that is alt text's job).
- No emoji in Pin titles — they displace keyword space and do not render reliably in
  search results.
- Destination URLs do not count toward the description limit; they are a separate
  field.

## LinkedIn

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Post | 3,000 characters |
| Article body | 110,000 characters |
| Article headline | 100 characters |
| Comment | 1,250 characters |
| Headline (profile) | 220 characters |
| About / summary | 2,600 characters |
| Company page tagline | 120 characters |
| Document (carousel) title | 150 characters |
| Image alt text | 300 characters |

### Truncation

- **Mobile:** approximately **140 characters** before "…see more". This is the
  binding constraint.
- **Desktop:** approximately **210 characters**.

Write the hook to land inside **140** so it works on both.

### Write to

**1,300–2,000 characters.** LinkedIn is the exception to the "shorter is better"
pattern — posts in this range earn measurably higher engagement than short ones,
because the platform rewards dwell time and the audience reads.

Structure:
1. **Line 1 (under 140 characters):** the hook. A claim, a tension, or a result.
2. **Line break.**
3. **Body:** short paragraphs, 1–2 sentences each, separated by blank lines.
4. **Close:** one question or one clear next step.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** no strict cap; 3 is the practical maximum before dilution.
- **Recommended:** 3. LinkedIn's own guidance is 3 relevant hashtags.
- **Placement:** end of the post, on their own line.

### Links and mentions

- External links in the post body suppress reach. Put the link in the first comment
  and say so in the post ("link in the comments").
- URLs count toward the 3,000 at full length.
- @mentions of people and companies are clickable and count toward the total.

### Rules

- No engagement bait ("comment YES below"). It is explicitly down-ranked.
- Blank lines are the formatting system. A post without them will not be read.
- Write in first person. Corporate third-person voice underperforms on this platform
  regardless of the account type.
- Document/carousel posts: the post caption still follows the 3,000-character rules
  above and still truncates at 140.

## YouTube

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Video title | 100 characters |
| Description | 5,000 characters |
| Shorts title | 100 characters |
| Shorts description | 5,000 characters |
| Tags (all combined) | 500 characters |
| Single tag | 30 characters |
| Hashtags per video | 15 (beyond 15, all are ignored) |
| Community post | 5,000 characters |
| Comment | 10,000 characters |
| Channel description | 1,000 characters |
| Playlist title | 150 characters |
| Playlist description | 5,000 characters |

### Truncation

- **Title:** truncates around **70 characters** in search results and around 40–60 on
  mobile home feed cards. Put the essential words first.
- **Description:** only the first **100–157 characters** show above the "…more" fold
  on the watch page. On Shorts, roughly the first **100**.

### Write to

- **Title:** 50–70 characters. Primary keyword in the first 40. No clickbait that the
  video does not deliver — retention loss outweighs the click gain.
- **Shorts title:** 40–60 characters, since Shorts titles are the only text most
  viewers see.
- **Description, first 150 characters:** a self-contained summary that works as a
  search snippet. This is the part that appears in Google and YouTube search results.
- **Description, full:** 200–500 words for a standard video — summary, timestamps,
  links, credits, and a keyword-rich paragraph. Use the space; it is indexed.

### Hashtags

- **Hard limit:** 15. Exceeding 15 causes YouTube to ignore *every* hashtag on the
  video, not just the excess.
- **Recommended:** 3–5.
- **Placement:** the first 3 hashtags in the description appear above the video title.
  Hashtags in the title also work but consume title characters — prefer the
  description.

### Rules

- Timestamps in the description create chapters. Chapters start at `0:00` and need at
  least three entries of at least 10 seconds each.
- Links in descriptions are clickable and count toward the 5,000.
- Tags matter little for ranking now but still help with misspellings and
  disambiguation. Do not burn effort on them.
- Never put a wall of hashtags at the top of a description — it displaces the search
  snippet.

## Google Business Profile

### Limits

| Field | Hard limit |
|---|---|
| Post ("What's new") | 1,500 characters |
| Event post description | 1,500 characters |
| Event title | 58 characters |
| Offer post title | 58 characters |
| Offer coupon code | 58 characters |
| Business description | 750 characters |
| Product name | 58 characters |
| Product description | 1,000 characters |
| Review reply | 4,096 characters |
| Q&A answer | 4,000 characters |

### Truncation

- **Google Maps:** as few as **100–150 characters** before "Read more". Maps is the
  most compressed surface.
- **Google Search:** approximately **250–300 characters**.

Assume 100 characters. Anything essential goes there.

### Write to

**150–300 characters.** GBP posts behave like local ads, not like social captions.

Structure:
1. **First 100 characters:** what is being offered and where, in plain language.
2. **Remaining 50–200:** the specifics — dates, price, conditions.
3. **A CTA button** (Book, Order, Learn more, Sign up, Call) — always attach one.
   The button is a separate field and does not consume characters.

### Post types

| Type | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| What's new | General updates, news | Expires after 6 months |
| Event | Dated happenings | Requires title (58 chars) + start/end dates |
| Offer | Promotions | Requires title (58 chars) + validity dates; supports coupon code, terms, and link |

### Hashtags

- **Do not use hashtags on Google Business Profile.** They are not clickable, carry no
  ranking signal, and consume the visible window.

### Rules

- Include the city or neighbourhood name in the first 100 characters — GBP posts are
  a local-search surface and location terms are the ranking signal.
- No promotional gimmicks, no ALL CAPS, no excessive punctuation. GBP posts are
  moderated and are rejected for spammy formatting.
- URLs are a separate button field. Do not paste a URL into the post body.
- Write plainly. This is the least "social" surface in the list — a customer searching
  for a business wants information, not a voice.
- Posts expire. "What's new" posts stop showing after 6 months; event and offer posts
  stop at their end date.

## When a caption does not fit

In priority order:

1. Cut adverbs, hedges, and throat-clearing openers ("We're excited to announce
   that…").
2. Cut the second idea. There should only be one.
3. Shorten the CTA, never the hook.
4. Move detail into the first comment (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn) or a reply
   (X) — never onto Pinterest or Google Business, which have no such surface.
5. Only then trim the hook — and if the hook has to be trimmed, the caption needs
   rewriting, not editing.

Never solve a length problem by removing line breaks. A caption that fits because it
is unreadable has not been fixed.
